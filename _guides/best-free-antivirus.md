Short answer: for most people, **no third-party antivirus is needed**. Microsoft
Defender, already built into Windows 10 and 11, scores at or near the top of
independent lab tests and costs nothing. What we do recommend adding is
**Malwarebytes** as an on-demand second opinion.

## The honest setup

1. **Keep Microsoft Defender on.** It is already there, already updated through
   Windows Update, and it does not nag or upsell you.
2. **Scan monthly (or when something feels off) with Malwarebytes Free.** It
   specializes in adware, browser hijackers and potentially unwanted programs,
   exactly the junk that slips past traditional antivirus.

## Why not Avast, AVG or Avira Free?

They detect malware fine, but the free versions exist to upsell you:
persistent notifications, scare-tactic "issues found" screens, and bundled
browser extensions. Avast was also fined for selling user browsing data through
its Jumpshot subsidiary in 2020. The value trade is no longer worth it when the
built-in option is this good.

## When paid antivirus does make sense

- You manage PCs for relatives who click everything
- A business machine with compliance requirements
- You want extras like a firewall manager, VPN or identity monitoring in one bill

Otherwise, save the money.

## FAQ

**Is Microsoft Defender really good enough?**
In AV-TEST and AV-Comparatives results it consistently sits alongside the paid
leaders for protection. A decade ago it was weak; that reputation is outdated.

**Malwarebytes found "PUPs" - is my PC infected?**
Potentially unwanted programs are usually toolbars, "driver updaters" and
similar junk rather than viruses. Let Malwarebytes remove them; nothing of
value is lost.

Short answer: **LibreOffice**, and it is not close. Both suites descend from
the same OpenOffice.org codebase, but since the 2010 split nearly all
development energy moved to LibreOffice. OpenOffice today receives mostly
maintenance releases; LibreOffice ships major updates twice a year.

## The differences that matter

**Microsoft format support:** LibreOffice reads and writes docx/xlsx/pptx
well. OpenOffice can open them but still **saves only the old doc/xls/ppt
formats**, which alone disqualifies it for anyone exchanging files in 2026.

**Development pace:** LibreOffice: two major releases a year, monthly fixes,
thousands of commits. OpenOffice: occasional maintenance releases from a small
team. Security response time follows the same pattern.

**Interface:** LibreOffice offers optional ribbon-style layouts alongside the
classic toolbars. OpenOffice keeps the 2005 look, which some genuinely prefer.

## Why does OpenOffice still exist?

Trademark history. The OpenOffice name went to the Apache Foundation while the
developer community moved to LibreOffice. The brand recognition of
"OpenOffice" keeps download numbers high, but momentum tells the real story.

## FAQ

**Will my old .odt files work in either?**
Yes, both use the OpenDocument format natively and are fully compatible with
each other's files.

**Is either fully compatible with complex Excel spreadsheets?**
LibreOffice Calc handles most real-world workbooks including many macros
(with Basic rewrites). Very heavy VBA or Power Query workbooks are still a
reason to keep Excel.

Short answer: use **qBittorrent**. uTorrent was the standard fifteen years ago,
but years of banner ads, bundled offers in the installer and even a bundled
crypto miner incident in 2015 burned that trust. qBittorrent gives you the same
familiar interface with none of it.

## Why qBittorrent won

- **No ads, ever.** It is open source and volunteer-run; there is nothing to
  monetize you with.
- **Built-in search** across multiple torrent sites, so you rarely need a
  browser.
- **Sequential download** lets you preview media while it downloads.
- **RSS auto-download** with filters: subscribe once, fetch new releases
  automatically.
- **Web UI** for controlling a home server or seedbox remotely.

## What about the others?

- **uTorrent**: tiny once, now ad-supported with offer screens in the installer.
  The classic client is no longer something we can recommend downloading.
- **Transmission**: beautifully minimal, great on Mac and Linux; the Windows
  port works but qBittorrent is more at home there.
- **Deluge**: capable and plugin-based, but development is slow and setup is
  fiddlier.

## FAQ

**Is BitTorrent itself legal?**
The protocol is completely legal and used to distribute Linux, game updates and
open data. Downloading copyrighted material without permission is not. The tool
is neutral; what you fetch with it is on you.

**Does qBittorrent have a mobile app?**
No official one, but the built-in Web UI works well from a phone browser, and
third-party remote apps support it.

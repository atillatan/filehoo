Short answer: it depends what "editing" means to you. **GIMP** for
Photoshop-style photo work, **Krita** for drawing and painting, **Paint.NET**
for fast simple edits. All three are free; none of them charges a subscription.

{% include guide-app.html url="/downloads/gimp/" badge="Photo editing" pitch="The closest free thing to Photoshop: layers, masks, healing tools, color curves and now non-destructive filters in version 3. Steeper learning curve, no subscription, ever." %}

GIMP 3 fixed the two biggest historical complaints: the interface got a real
modernization and filters are finally non-destructive. It opens PSD files well
enough for most real-world use.

{% include guide-app.html url="/downloads/krita/" badge="Drawing & painting" pitch="Built by artists for artists: brush engines and stabilizers that rival paid illustration apps, plus a 2D animation timeline. If your input is a pen tablet, choose Krita over GIMP." %}

The distinction people miss: GIMP manipulates existing images; Krita is for
creating images from a blank canvas. Krita's brush feel is in a different
league.

## Also worth knowing

- **Paint.NET**: the sweet spot between MS Paint and GIMP. Layers and plugins,
  loads instantly, ideal for crops, resizes and quick fixes. (Free from
  getpaint.net; the Microsoft Store version is paid to support development.)
- **Photopea**: a shockingly complete Photoshop clone that runs in your
  browser, ad-supported. Great in a pinch or on a locked-down PC.

## Head to head

| | GIMP | Krita | Paint.NET |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best at | Photo retouch | Painting | Quick edits |
| PSD support | Good | Basic | Via plugin |
| Learning curve | Steep | Moderate | Easy |
| Pen tablet feel | OK | Excellent | Basic |

## FAQ

**Can GIMP fully replace Photoshop?**
For hobby and most freelance work, yes. Hard blockers tend to be CMYK print
workflows and tight PSD round-tripping with clients.

**Is Krita only for anime-style art?**
No, that is just its most visible community. The brushes handle any style, and
it is also a capable texture-painting tool.
